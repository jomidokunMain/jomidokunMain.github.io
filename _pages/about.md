# Human-Centered AI for Intelligent Transportation and Ergonomics

Hello, I'm **Jesudara Omidokun** — you can call me **Dara**. I am a Ph.D. student in **Industrial and Manufacturing Systems Engineering** at the **University of Michigan–Dearborn**, advised by Professor **Bochen Jia**.

My work focuses on how people interact with intelligent technologies. I combine **human factors engineering**, **machine learning**, **computer vision**, **statistical modeling**, and **physiological/behavioral sensing** to study trust, safety, workload, and user experience in automated and data-driven systems.

My long-term goal is to develop **human-centered intelligent systems** that can understand human behavior, adapt to user needs, and support safer interaction with technologies such as automated vehicles, human-machine interfaces, wearable systems, and digital human models.

**Current research focus:** Human Digital Twins and adaptive transparency for automated vehicle interaction.

## Research Identity

I describe my research using one central question:

> **How can intelligent systems better understand, support, and adapt to humans in complex environments?**

This question connects my work across automated driving, driver behavior, ergonomics, human motion analysis, and machine-learning-based sensing. Rather than treating these as separate projects, I view them as part of a broader effort to build **human-centered AI systems** that improve safety, trust calibration, and human performance.

---

## Research Themes

### 1. Human-Centered AI and Automated Driving

I study how drivers and occupants understand, trust, and respond to automated vehicle systems. This includes trust transfer across automation levels, driver reliance, adaptive human-machine interfaces, and transparency strategies that help users build accurate mental models of system capability.

**Keywords:** automated vehicles, trust calibration, adaptive transparency, HMI, behavioral reliance, driver monitoring.

### 2. Human Digital Twins and User-State Modeling

I am developing a research direction around **Human Digital Twins** for modeling human states such as trust, workload, attention, posture, and physiological response. The goal is to support intelligent systems that can personalize feedback and transparency based on the user's state and task context.

**Keywords:** human digital twin, physiological sensing, cognitive load, trust, user modeling, adaptive systems.

### 3. Computer Vision and Biomechanical Assessment

My earlier work uses computer vision, digital human modeling, and motion analysis to support ergonomic and biomechanical assessment. This includes contactless human motion analysis, workload estimation, joint force assessment, and ground reaction force estimation using machine learning.

**Keywords:** computer vision, 3D human reconstruction, pose estimation, ergonomics, biomechanics, motion analysis.

### 4. Data-Driven Human Factors and Experimental Analytics

Across projects, I use experimental design and statistical modeling to analyze human behavior and system performance. My interests include mixed-effects modeling, regression, structural equation modeling, machine learning pipelines, and interpretable analysis for human-subject studies.

**Keywords:** statistics, experimental design, mixed-effects models, SEM, Python, MATLAB, behavioral data analysis.

## Featured Projects

### Human Digital Twin for Adaptive Transparency in Automated Vehicles

**Problem.** Automated vehicle users often struggle to understand system capability, responsibility, and limitations across automation levels.

**Approach.** I am exploring how human digital twin models can combine behavioral, subjective, and physiological indicators to support adaptive transparency and improve trust calibration.

**Methods.** Human factors theory, trust modeling, physiological sensing, survey design, statistical modeling, and human-machine interface evaluation.

**Status.** Dissertation research direction in development.

---

### Trust Transfer Across Automation Levels

**Problem.** When a driver moves between different automation levels or interface designs, trust can be overestimated, underestimated, or transferred inappropriately.

**Approach.** This project examines how HMI similarity, perceived continuity, mental models, and responsibility attribution influence trust transfer between automated driving modes.

**Methods.** Experimental survey design, trust and acceptance measures, structural equation modeling, behavioral reliance measures, and transition-comprehension checks.

**Status.** Active research project.

---

### Data-Driven Driver Behavior and ADAS Analysis

**Problem.** Automated and assisted driving systems require careful evaluation of driver attention, interaction patterns, and control behavior.

**Approach.** I analyze vehicle signals, gaze behavior, and assistance-system interactions to understand how drivers use and respond to proactive driving support.

**Methods.** Python/MATLAB data processing, gaze analysis, vehicle-signal analysis, statistical modeling, visualization, and human factors interpretation.

**Status.** Active research analysis pipeline.

---

### FitCheck Ergonomics and Pressure Mapping Pipeline

**Problem.** Ergonomic chair evaluation requires combining subjective comfort ratings, pressure mapping, posture behavior, and participant-level experimental data.

**Approach.** I support data cleaning, dictionary development, and analysis pipelines for chair trials and pressure-mat measurements.

**Methods.** Data cleaning, pressure-map feature extraction, posture-shift metrics, visualization, and statistical summaries.

**Status.** Ongoing collaborative study.

---

### Scaled Autonomous Vehicle Platform

**Problem.** Autonomous driving education and research require flexible platforms where machine-learning-based driving algorithms can be tested safely.

**Approach.** My master's thesis developed a scaled autonomous vehicle platform with drive-by-wire capability, sensing, and deep-learning-based system design.

**Methods.** Embedded systems, C++/Python, computer vision, machine learning, vehicle control, and autonomous-system integration.

**Outputs.** Thesis, video demonstration, and code repository.
