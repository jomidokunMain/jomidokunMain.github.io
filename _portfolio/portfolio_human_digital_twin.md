## Overview

This project is part of my emerging dissertation direction on **Human Digital Twins for automated vehicle interaction**. The goal is to model how users understand, trust, and rely on intelligent driving systems, then use that understanding to support adaptive transparency and safer human-automation interaction.

## Problem

Automated vehicle users often struggle to understand what the system can do, when they are responsible, and how much they should trust the system. This problem becomes more complex as automation shifts across levels, modes, and interface designs.

A human digital twin can help represent the user's state over time by integrating behavioral, subjective, and physiological indicators.

## Research Questions

- How can human-state models represent trust, workload, attention, and reliance during automated driving?
- Which subjective, behavioral, and physiological indicators best capture user state across automation levels?
- How can adaptive transparency support better trust calibration and safer interaction?

## My Contribution

- Developing the conceptual framework for a human-centered digital twin system.
- Connecting trust, workload, attention, and reliance measures into a unified modeling direction.
- Designing the research structure for future human-subject studies and adaptive HMI evaluation.
